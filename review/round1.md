## A. Who speaks

Everything outside quotation marks is the narrator's (F-2, old). Of the 62 quoted lines, 60 are settled by a speech tag
or the context. These six items are the two the text leaves open, plus four choices about how the lines are heard.

**A1. p036**: *"We can readily solve the hidden secret of their force-screen."* No tag. Roal has just finished, and the
next line is "He was interrupted", so it's a man speaking.
*Recommend:* **Roal**, carrying on. The alternatives are Trest, who leads the work on the beams, or Kahsh.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**A2. p101**: *"Why should they? Earth was the planet for which they were best fitted."* No tag. It comes between Roal's
"Pity that men would not spread to other planets," and Trest's "We are alive—but is it worth it?… answered Trest."
*Recommend:* **Roal**, answering his own regret. That way "answered Trest" in the next line answers him. The alternative
is Trest pushing back, who would then speak twice running.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**A3. Roal's first speech (p033–p035).** It's introduced as "It was Roal who sent the first thoughts among the gathered
men", but it goes on "he began" and has his chuckle in it.
*Recommend:* **spoken aloud**. I read "thoughts" as "ideas" here.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**A4. Thought-speech.** The text says these lines arrive as thought, not sound: the newborn F-2's first words (p062, p064),
F-2's vow to the last men (p108–p111), the first force-sphere (p121), and the golden sphere throughout (p125, p128,
p132–p135). They get the shimmer in the mix and the rings of light on screen (PLAN.md §4–5). Everything else is spoken,
including F-2's ordinary lines and the machines' (C-R-21's "vibrationally correct, emotionless tones" settle it that
machines have voices).
*Recommend:* as marked.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**A5. The Outsiders in Roal's mouth (p034).** Roal imagines them saying *'The life-form of Earth is sending out
controlled machines. We will find good machines we can use.'*
*Recommend:* **Roal's own voice**, a little flatter, as a man does when he quotes someone. A second voice would suggest
the Outsiders really said it.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**A6. "Power ready" (p116).** It's "the signal of the small brain built into" the power plant: a fourteenth speaker, with
two words.
*Recommend:* its own voice, the smallest and flattest of the machine family. (The alternative is to give it to C-R-U-1's
voice.)
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

## B. How the words are said

These are on the listening page, as spelled and as each option would say them. Respellings change only what the voice
is sent. The script, captions and site keep Campbell's spelling, and each designation is lettered on screen the first
time it's spoken.

**B1. Designations.** Option A is in pairs ("X fifty-six thirty-eight", "Roal, twenty-five, three seventy-four"); option
B is digit by digit ("X five six three eight", "two five three seven four").
*Recommend:* **B**. They're serial numbers, and digit by digit says so. The long ones (Roal's, Trest's and Kahsh's) are
each spoken only once.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**B2. Kahsh.** Option A rhymes with *wash* ("Kosh"); option C rhymes with *cash*.
*Recommend:* **Kosh**. The *ah* in the spelling points that way.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**B3. 2538.** "Twenty-five thirty-eight", or "two thousand five hundred and thirty-eight".
*Recommend:* **twenty-five thirty-eight**. It's a date, and the second form is long.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**B4. Roal.** One syllable ("Role"), or two ("Ro-al").
*Recommend:* **one syllable**. The *oa* is the *oa* of *coal*.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**B5. Kilads.** "Kill-ads" or "Kye-lads".
*Recommend:* **kill-ads**. Footnote 1 makes it a machine unit, so it's like *kilo*.
**Curt:** accepted (2026-11-01)

**B6. Everything else.** These have one form each: *mentatype* (menta-type), *Deimos* (Dye-mos), C-R-U-1, F-1 and F-2.
Listen for anything the voice gets wrong that I haven't caught.

## D. Runtime (nothing to decide yet)

The scratch voices read at **153 words a minute**, narration and dialogue alike. That makes the film **about 47
minutes**, before any holds for pictures. F-2's narrator is meant to be unhurried and very old. At about 135 words a
minute it would run 52 to 55 minutes, which is what the plan expects. The pace gets set by ear in Phase 3.
