# Round 4: the look

Phase 4 runs beside casting (PLAN.md §9), so this round is open alongside Round 3. It asks one thing first: is the
medium right? The style frames test the plan's one rule (§5): the world in ink wash on aged pulp paper, in warm greys
and sepia, with colour only for energy, in the colours the text names. The ships, machines and people in them are
placeholders. Their model sheets come next, once the medium is settled, with the QR layouts and the title page.

**To look:** [the style frames](https://curtcox.github.io/The-Last-Evolution/review/round4/) on the site, or locally
after `npm start` (http://localhost:8077/review/round4/). Each still opens full size. In the studio
(http://localhost:8077/studio/?loop=look1, through `look6` and `lookTitle`) you can scrub them.

The gate closes when the designs and a QR layout are approved (PLAN.md §9). This round settles the medium; the designs
follow in the next.

---

## 1. The medium

**1.1 The paper and the wash.** Yellowed pulp stock with fibres, foxing and browned edges; washes in five dilutions of
one sepia ink, with a pen line where a shape needs one; the line boils at 12 drawings a second (the clip on the page).
Say what's wrong: warmer or cooler, darker or lighter, looser washes, more or less line.
*Recommend:* approve it as the base, and judge the details on the model sheets, where there's more to look at.
**Curt:**

**1.2 The halftone.** The magazine printed its washes through a coarse dot screen. The page shows frame 2 with none, a
light screen (the one the frames use) and a strong one.
*Recommend:* **light.** It reads as print without fighting the energy, which is kept out of the screen.
**Curt:**

## 2. Energy, the only colour

**2.1 Energy as light.** Every beam, aura and streamer is laid as added light over a thin wash of its colour, so it
glows on the dark grounds and never mixes into the ink. The world around it stays in the sepia greys.
*Recommend:* approve.
**Curt:**

**2.2 The screens under fire** (frame 3). The text says a screen "flamed gradually violet, then blue, orange—red". In
the film that's a sequence in time. The frame also spreads it in space: red where a beam lands, through orange and blue,
to violet where the fire hasn't reached.
*Recommend:* both, as in the frame: the hit glows hotter as the fire goes on, and the rest of the screen follows.
**Curt:**

**2.3 The colour arc** (frames 1 to 6): grey for 2538; green death across the grey; the screens' violet to red; F-2's
birth in white and blue; the golden sphere, the first warm colour that isn't a weapon; and the frame, 125,000 years on,
the only scene in full colour.
*Recommend:* keep the arc as the plan has it. The frames show it works step by step.
**Curt:**

## 3. Lettering

**3.1 The chapter-title page.** The running head, THE LAST EVOLUTION in spaced capitals over a double rule, then the
chapter's title in italic and its opening paragraph with a drop cap, in Old Standard TT, a revival of the period's
book face. It holds at each chapter's head while the motif plays (Round 3, 3.1).
*Recommend:* approve the layout. One choice remains: whether the page shows the opening paragraph (as in the frame) or
only the title. Showing it lets the narrator start reading over the page, as if from it.
**Curt:**
