# Round 4: the look

Phase 4 runs beside casting (PLAN.md §9), so this round is open alongside Round 3. It asks one thing first: is the
medium right? The style frames test the plan's one rule (§5): the world in ink wash on aged pulp paper, in warm greys
and sepia, with colour only for energy, in the colours the text names. The ships, machines and people in them are
placeholders. Their model sheets come next, once the medium is settled, with the QR layouts and the title page.

**To look:** [the style frames](https://curtcox.github.io/The-Last-Evolution/review/round4/) on the site, or locally
after `npm start` (http://localhost:8077/review/round4/). Each still opens full size. In the studio
(http://localhost:8077/studio/?loop=look1, through `look6` and `lookTitle`) you can scrub them.

The gate closes when the designs and a QR layout are approved (PLAN.md §9). This round settles the medium; the designs
follow in the next.

---

## 1. The medium

**1.1 The paper and the wash.** Yellowed pulp stock with fibres, foxing and browned edges; washes in five dilutions of
one sepia ink, with a pen line where a shape needs one; the line boils at 12 drawings a second (the clip on the page).
Say what's wrong: warmer or cooler, darker or lighter, looser washes, more or less line.
*Recommend:* approve it as the base, and judge the details on the model sheets, where there's more to look at.
**Curt:** use your recommendations.

**1.2 The halftone.** The magazine printed its washes through a coarse dot screen. The page shows frame 2 with none, a
light screen (the one the frames use) and a strong one.
*Recommend:* **light.** It reads as print without fighting the energy, which is kept out of the screen.
**Curt:** use your recommendations.

## 2. Energy, the only colour

**2.1 Energy as light.** Every beam, aura and streamer is laid as added light over a thin wash of its colour, so it
glows on the dark grounds and never mixes into the ink. The world around it stays in the sepia greys.
*Recommend:* approve.
**Curt:** use your recommendations.

**2.2 The screens under fire** (frame 3). The text says a screen "flamed gradually violet, then blue, orange—red". In
the film that's a sequence in time. The frame also spreads it in space: red where a beam lands, through orange and blue,
to violet where the fire hasn't reached.
*Recommend:* both, as in the frame: the hit glows hotter as the fire goes on, and the rest of the screen follows.
**Curt:** use your recommendations.

**2.3 The colour arc** (frames 1 to 6): grey for 2538; green death across the grey; the screens' violet to red; F-2's
birth in white and blue; the golden sphere, the first warm colour that isn't a weapon; and the frame, 125,000 years on,
the only scene in full colour.
*Recommend:* keep the arc as the plan has it. The frames show it works step by step.
**Curt:** use your recommendations.

## 3. Lettering

**3.1 The chapter-title page.** The running head, THE LAST EVOLUTION in spaced capitals over a double rule, then the
chapter's title in italic and its opening paragraph with a drop cap, in Old Standard TT, a revival of the period's
book face. It holds at each chapter's head while the motif plays (Round 3, 3.1).
*Recommend:* approve the layout. One choice remains: whether the page shows the opening paragraph (as in the frame) or
only the title. Showing it lets the narrator start reading over the page, as if from it.
**Curt:** use your recommendations.
**Claude:** the paragraph, then: each chapter's page shows its opening paragraph, and the narrator starts reading over
it.

## 5. The model sheets

**To look:** [the model sheets](https://curtcox.github.io/The-Last-Evolution/review/round4/models.html), or locally
at http://localhost:8077/review/round4/models.html (studio: `?loop=model1` through `model6`). This is a first pass at
every design in §5. Each one is built from the text, and the words it's built from are on the sheet. Where the text is
silent, I've invented something, and each invention is named below so you can strike it. The designs live in
`src/designs.js`, so the chapters draw the same machine every time.

**5.1 One family of machines** (sheet 2). The text gives every machine "the usual driving and lifting units", a
"dome-case", "force-beam controls" and "tentacles... built on the framework" (p048). It also calls F-1 "the shining
cylinder" (p058). So every machine of 2538 is drawn as a cylinder with a domed top, carried on a lifting disc, with its
tentacles hung from the framework between. The council's four are told apart by build: X-5638 slim and new, X-6349
with a probe, X-6621 with nozzles, C-R-21 tall with a crown of receptors. Each has its designation stencilled on its
band. F-1 is the same form, shining and vast. The power plant (sheet 6) is the same form again at five hundred feet,
since F-2 builds it.
*Recommend:* approve the family. It's the biggest design decision here, and it comes from the text. The stencilled
designations are my invention, so the audience can tell the machines apart.
**Curt:**

**5.2 The people** (sheet 1). Tunics, belts, boots and the high standing collar, as the pulps drew the future. The text
describes none of them, so these are inventions: Roal's long robe and short white beard (he's a hundred and fifty),
Trest's dark hair, and Kahsh's mantle, tall collar and disc of office.
*Recommend:* approve the costume. The figures themselves are stiff and need more life before the pilot: hands, poses,
the turn of a head. Say which inventions to drop.
**Curt:**

**5.3 F-2 and C-R-U-1** (sheet 3). F-2 is a small torpedo with a blunt nose, a lens and three thin tentacles from its
underside, in a faint blue. That blue is the one colour in the world that isn't energy, because the text says "bluish
metal". It goes grey-brown with age, and the old F-2 is rusted. C-R-U-1 has no disc and no tentacles, since the text
gives it none and it works by force. Its name glows in gold.
*Recommend:* keep F-2's faint blue, as the text gives it. It makes the narrator recognisable in every scene. Also
approve C-R-U-1 without tentacles.
**Curt:**

**5.4 The Outsiders** (sheet 4). "Three of them, set at equal distances apart" can mean three eyes in a row. I've read
it as three eyes around the crown of the head, a third of a turn apart, since the same sentence says the eyes are
"capable of movement in every direction". The text makes them about four feet tall: short, squat and armoured in horny
brown plates. The suit is drawn as a clear outline with a glint.
*Recommend:* eyes around the crown. Like the people, the figures need more life and less armour-plate stiffness
before the pilot.
**Curt:**

**5.5 Ships, investigators, motes and places** (sheets 5 and 6).
- The torpedo of the void's projector turrets and drive bells are inventions; the text gives only a torpedo, a thousand
  kilads long and shining dully.
- The investigator is a three-inch sphere with a ring of receptors and short feelers. The feelers are an invention, to
  let it "creep" and "worm".
- The motes, the ten thousand tiny machine-ships, are the family form laid on its side. That's an invention: the text
  only calls them tiny, darting motes.
- The black dome is shown cut away, with F-2's machine and the violet beam that holds it up (p095, p097).
- The laboratory's hall of piers and screens is an invention around the screens the text gives.

*Recommend:* approve, with the motes and the laboratory as the items most open to change.
