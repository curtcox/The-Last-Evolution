# Before you watch

*The foreword to the annotated film, shown above it on the watch page. Draft for Round 7. Sources are in
[refs.yaml](refs.yaml).*

This is John W. Campbell, Jr.'s *The Last Evolution*, from *Amazing Stories* for August 1932, read in full. Every word
of it is spoken, in order, by a cast of voices, over paintings in the manner of the magazine's own ink illustrations. It
runs about an hour, in twelve chapters.

The story is a machine's memoir. Its first line tells you the machine is the last of its kind; the rest tells you how
it came to be, and what came after mankind.

## When it was written

Campbell was 22 and finishing a physics degree ([SFE][sfe-campbell]). 1932 was the year the atom came apart: the
neutron was found in February, the first atom split by a machine in April, and the positron, the first antimatter,
photographed on 2 August, the month this story was on the stands ([discovery of the neutron][wp-neutron]). There was no
electronic computer anywhere. When the story says "machine", it's imagining something no one had built.

## How to watch

Notes appear beside the film as it reaches the words they're about: about one a minute, never over the picture, and
never stopping the film. There are six kinds, and you can turn any of them off:

- **Who's who**, for the people and machines, and the numbers they go by;
- **Words**, for the story's own terms and numbers;
- **1932 science**, for what Campbell knew, and what we know now;
- **The text**, for its two printings, its footnotes and its illustration;
- **Heirs**, for the idea at its heart, that machines might inherit from us, before Campbell and since;
- **The making**, for where the film pictures what the text leaves unsaid.

If it's your first time, you might watch with the notes off and read them afterwards, all listed below the film, as
the story's first readers met it: with nothing in the margin.

## A word on the words

The story keeps its 1932 language: "Man" for humanity, "the Year of the Son of Man", "kilads". One speech calls
mankind a parasite. It's there whole, because the film keeps every word, and because the story's last page argues with
it.

## Who made this

The voices are made with ElevenLabs, each designed for its part; none is a real person's. The film and these notes
were made by Curt Cox with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. An AI helping to tell a 1932 story about machines
inheriting the Earth is an irony we'd rather name than hide.
